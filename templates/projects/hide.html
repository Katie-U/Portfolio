The team

Michigan Tech's Enterprise programme puts students on long-running teams with real clients and real deadlines. I joined HIDE, the Humane Interface Design Enterprise, which does software development and usability research.

I worked on the human factors team — the group the other teams came to for interface and usability work. Two of those projects are below.

CS Scheduler

The computer science department wanted a better way to schedule classes and to see the data while they worked. They had been using spreadsheets, where conflicts between professors or courses are effectively invisible until someone spots them. Our team was asked to prototype what an interface might look like instead.

We interviewed the development team and the department staff who actually do the scheduling, then designed to what we heard. The layout deliberately echoes Google Calendar, because that is the tool the staff already know — and laying the schedule out in time makes the conflicts show up on their own.

The screen shows a single professor's schedule; the calendar shows several at once. This is an early design, not the shipped one — I moved to another team before the project finished.

Legal paper serving platform

This app supports people who serve legal papers for a living. I produced the first wireframe prototype: a proof of concept for the client and a direction for the development team to build against.

The design came out of interviews with the client, who both served papers himself and managed a team doing the same. It works as a website and as a mobile app, with the mobile side carrying the job of recording proof that papers were served. I moved to another project before the designs were finalised.
