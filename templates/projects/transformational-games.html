Designing on a two-week clock

Transformational games are games built to change something in the player. The course covered how to design them, working in physical formats — dice, cards, and other mechanics you can hold.

Every design ran on a roughly two-week cycle, start to finish. That constraint is the real lesson: with two weeks you cannot test everything, so you learn to decide what a round of playtesting is actually for and to cut the rest.

The team game

Our group designed a game about climate issues, built to make players argue a position that is not their own. The aim is to give people practice at difficult climate conversations with someone who disagrees with them or does not follow their reasoning.

Each player holds a role with secret goals to hit while the table works toward the best solution to a shared problem — so cooperating and pursuing your own agenda pull against each other by design.

Designing it as a team was its own lesson in how an idea shifts once more than one person owns it, from brainstorming through to the final rules.
