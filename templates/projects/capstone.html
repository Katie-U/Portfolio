The brief

Every team in the masters programme spends a year with an outside client. Mine worked with South Fayette High School in McDonald, Pennsylvania.

Students there get funnelled into a single subject area long before they reach college, and the choice is usually made for them. Our client wanted the opposite: more student autonomy, and enough internal motivation for students to choose their own path forward — college, a trade, or straight into work.

Research

We started by mapping the problem space: a competitive analysis of other school districts, and background reading on how the shape of American secondary education got this way.

From there we interviewed the people who live in it — students, parents, teachers and administrators — to understand what each group actually wants and what motivates them. Participatory design sessions gave us our client's own vision in their words. We presented the findings back and used their response to set the direction for the next phase.

Prototyping

We built prototypes to test assumptions rather than to demonstrate a finished idea: interviews, Wizard of Oz studies standing in for AI recommendations, and cardboard mock-ups.

Each one was cheap enough to throw away and pointed at a single question. What we learned across them is what the final design is built on.

The solution

Stacks helps students record what they have done and then use that record to spot patterns and options they had not considered. The same history can be read as a timeline or clustered by similarity, and it covers extracurriculars and outside opportunities alongside coursework — so the picture is of the whole student, not just their transcript.

I built the database holding student, class, extracurricular and event records, and helped develop the recommendation system that suggests next steps from a student's interests and past experience.
